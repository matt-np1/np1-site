Why Uniformitys exists

In Kabokweni, Mpumalanga, thousands of families need school uniforms every year, and there is nowhere local to buy them. Parents travel to town and try to match colours and badges themselves, or buy from a few informal sellers.

Sphiwe Masinga grew up with this. He saw a simple product that the schools around him needed, and that nobody was supplying.

Sphiwe with uniform samples, Kabokweni.

Why we backed him

Sphiwe is the classic township founder N+1 exists to back. The business is no rocket science: good uniforms, made close to the schools, sold straight to families. What he had was the market, the school relationships and the drive to do the work.

At the end of 2024, N+1 put in R120k so he could buy stock and leave his call-centre job. Since then we have supported him with strategy, accounts, brand and design while he built the business school by school.

Where it is now

Learners at one of the schools Uniformitys now serves.

Four schools are fully on board, and Uniformitys has sold to a handful of others, but it can supply only about 10% of their learners, because bought-in stock from Johannesburg takes up to three months.

The next 5 or 6 schools are ready for a full ramp-up, and 23 schools have expressed real intent to make Uniformitys their supplier. Sphiwe has proved the demand and started making locally. He is ready for the next step.
